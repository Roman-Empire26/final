The fluidity of the process comes from the relationship between the creator and their tools. Each tool can change the direction, technique, and outcome of the work. The process of creating is therefore not completely fixed, because the tools used by the creator can influence how an idea is developed and represented. This connects to Andrés Bello's discussion of the "progress of the art of writing," where progress can be understood through movement and changes in the tools used for writing. The theory of externalization also helps explain this relationship. As tools are removed from the body, the process of writing changes. What was once directly connected to the body can become separated through the use of an external tool. This creates a fluid relationship between the creator, the tool, and the final product. Rather than viewing a tool as simply something used to complete a task, we can see how the tool participates in the process itself. The tool can therefore influence not only how something is created, but also how the resulting work takes shape.
