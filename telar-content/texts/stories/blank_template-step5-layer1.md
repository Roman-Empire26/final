---
title: "Learn more"
---

Belief can change through the process of encountering, reading, interpreting, and practicing an idea. Rodríguez shows that people can interact with written knowledge in different ways: some simply look at it, while others examine, compile, or judge the ideas within it. The tool provides access to the idea, but the process of interpretation determines what the person takes from it. This means that the creation of an object is not the end of the process. Once an idea is represented through a particular tool, it can be encountered by different people and understood in different ways. Education and experience influence how a person interprets what they encounter, while practice can reinforce or change that understanding. The material form of an idea therefore becomes part of the process of producing meaning. What begins as an idea moves through a series of interactions between the creator, the tool, the social environment, and the audience. The process remains fluid because each interaction can change how the idea is understood.
