The Codex Mendoza shows how communication can change when knowledge moves between different cultures. Indigenous images and symbols were used alongside written explanations that helped a Spanish audience understand the information. This means the knowledge was not simply copied into another form. It was adjusted through different tools and methods of communication. The audience became part of the process because the information had to be presented in a way that could be understood by people using another system. This connects to the fluidity of process because knowledge can change form as it moves between people, cultures, and tools.
