---
title: "Learn more"
---

Tools give us different ways to communicate and preserve ideas. Written messages can act as substitutes for spoken communication, allowing ideas to move beyond the moment they were spoken. The use of writing and print allows ideas to travel across time and reach a larger public, but the tool also affects how an idea is received and understood. In the passage from Plato's Phaedrus, Socrates questions whether the written word can function in the same way as spoken communication. Written words remain after the speaker is gone, but they cannot respond or change according to the person reading them. This shows that writing does more than preserve an idea; it changes the relationship between the creator, the message, and the audience. The tool creates new possibilities for communication while also changing the process of how meaning is transmitted. Writing therefore becomes more than a substitute for speech — it becomes a different way of organizing and experiencing communication.
