Mexican codices are pictorials that document the history of the pre-Hispanics (more specifically, the Mexicas, Mayas, and Mixtecs). They used their tools to write on bark paper or deer skin, but other materials were also used, just not as frequently. According to Gordon Brotherston in 1992 "...describes the essential characteristics of codices as non-phonetic, although some might record concept-sounds, such as those produced by the Mayas." This way of "writing" is highly customizable in its presentation. You are not anchored to simple words; you can tell a story through an image of sorts. You can also integrate math; it is not just a way to speak with words as images. This way of writing breaks this notion of words being the only way to communicate.
