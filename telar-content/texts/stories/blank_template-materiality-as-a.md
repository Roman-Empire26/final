---
title: "Materiality as a Driving Force of Meaning"
---

Writing/communcating properties are never neutral containers for thought. They actively dictate how human expression takes shape. For instance, the texture of knots, the time and patience, the meaning of symbols, the rigid surface of prepared animal hide, and the boundaries of paper and ink don't just hold information, they govern cognitive processing, structural narrative, and historical record keeping. For example, Khipu: by relying on fiber composition, spin direction, and spatial knotting, it encoded complex quantitative and narrative meanings, requiring a visual and hands on approach far removed from the linear page. The Mixtec Codex Selden/Añute used gesso coated animal hide to force a meandering, boustrophedon reading path, locking historical memory into a dynamic way of retaining information. For the Nueva Corónica, when Felipe Guamán Poma de Ayala later turned to European paper and ink, the physical page turned into a battleground where alphabetic text and Andean spatial iconology directly collided. These three objects show that physical limits aren't just obstacles, they are catalysts for creative and political expansion. This material reality connects directly back to how writing technologies/evolution impose physical boundaries on communication, those exact constraints push creators/writers to innovate through nontraditional ways, tactile setups, and visual subversion. By wrestling with the physical boundaries of cordage, hide, paper, paint, etc. these creators/writers worked around alphabetic biases to reshape memory, claim authority, and rewrite public discourse.
